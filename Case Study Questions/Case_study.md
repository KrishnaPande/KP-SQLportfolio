## ✅ CASE 1 — Revenue dropped by 12%

**Answer frame (say this):**

> “First, I’d validate whether this is a real drop by confirming the time comparison and ruling out abnormal baselines like promotions or holidays.
>
> Then I’d decompose revenue into users, purchase frequency, and average order value to identify which driver changed.
>
> Once I know the driver, I’d segment by user type, product category, and geography to localize the issue.
>
> Finally, I’d check for data or tracking issues and form a hypothesis on the most likely cause before recommending next steps.”

---

## ✅ CASE 2 — A/B test is statistically significant but revenue dropped

**Answer frame:**

> “Statistical significance tells me the change is real, but not whether it’s good for the business.
>
> I’d first identify the primary metric the experiment was designed to optimize and treat other metrics as guardrails.
>
> Then I’d assess the magnitude of the revenue drop versus the observed lift and segment results to see if the impact is concentrated in specific user groups.
>
> Based on the trade-off, I’d decide whether to stop, iterate, or partially roll out the change.”

---

## ✅ CASE 3 — Define success for a new pricing feature

**Answer frame:**

> “I’d start by clarifying the business goal of the pricing feature.
>
> Based on that goal, I’d define a primary success metric such as revenue per user or average order value, along with guardrail metrics like conversion rate or churn.
>
> I’d then compare performance between users exposed to the new pricing and those who aren’t over a stable time window.
>
> Finally, I’d segment results to understand where the feature works or creates trade-offs before deciding on rollout.”

---

## ✅ CASE 4 — DAU is flat but session duration dropped

**Answer frame:**

> “Flat DAU tells me users are still coming, but lower session duration suggests a change in how they’re engaging.
>
> I’d hypothesize whether users are completing tasks faster, encountering friction, or disengaging earlier.
>
> To validate this, I’d segment by user type, feature usage, and recent product changes.
>
> Based on the pattern, I’d recommend whether this reflects healthy efficiency or a product experience issue.”

---

## ✅ CASE 5 — Dashboard shows a sudden spike

**Answer frame:**

> “Before assuming the spike is real, I’d first validate data integrity by checking for recent pipeline, logging, or definition changes.
>
> I’d compare the metric across related dashboards or external signals to see if the trend is consistent.
>
> If the spike holds after validation, I’d investigate what changed in the product or user behavior.
>
> Otherwise, I’d flag it as a data issue to prevent incorrect decisions.”
